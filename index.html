THE GOVERNING RULE
Know exactly what the credit is funding and when it gets repaid — before you draw it.
If you can answer with a specific revenue event and a specific date, the draw is probably defensible. If the honest answer is "I'll figure out the repayment once business picks up," you are not using credit as a bridge. You are using it as hope. This worksheet is calibrated to keep you on the right side of that line.

FACILITY 01 · LINE OF CREDIT
Your business or personal line of credit, if any.

Most flexible, lowest-cost. Best set up before you need it. If you don't have one, leave the balance at 0 — your assignment will include setting one up.

FACILITY 02 · CREDIT CARDS
Business and personal cards used for the business.

Combined balance across all cards used for business expenses. At 18–24% APR, the cost compounds faster than owners notice.

Money owed past the next statement. Not the spend you'll pay off this month.

YOUR INTEREST COST
The single most important number in this worksheet.

Pull your statements. Add up every dollar of interest you paid last month across every facility. This is the diagnostic the lesson hinges on.

Add it up across every facility — line, cards, RBF fees. If you have no debt, leave at 0.

STEP THREE · YOUR THREE RED LINES

Decide now. Not when payroll is due in 48 hours.

The reason discipline breaks down is that owners set credit rules under pressure. Decide them here, in writing, calmly. The output will be a one-page policy you keep visible.

RED LINE 01 · MAXIMUM TOTAL EXPOSURE
The most you'll let yourself owe — period.

Not what you can qualify for. What you're willing to be personally responsible for if the business doesn't recover. Anything above this is not a bridge — it's a bet.

A common discipline: no more than 3 months of personal essential expenses (mortgage, utilities, food). If your essentials run $8K/month, your ceiling is $24K. The number that matters is what you can survive losing — not what a lender will offer.

RED LINE 02 · THE SG&A RULE
What credit is allowed to cover — and what it's not.

The lesson is direct: credit does not touch payroll. Credit does not touch rent. Violating this once means giving yourself permission to do it again, and the boundary is gone permanently.

"Inventory tied to a confirmed customer order. Equipment with a documented productivity ROI. Bridge-funding a receivable already invoiced and confirmed."

"Payroll. Rent. Software subscriptions. Owner draw. Marketing without a tested conversion model. Anything I can't tie to a confirmed revenue event."

RED LINE 03 · THE REPAYMENT TRIGGER
Every draw has a named revenue event that retires it. And a date.

Define your default rule for how this works. If a future draw doesn't fit this rule, you don't make it.

e.g., 60 days, 90 days. The shorter, the safer. If you can't repay within 90 days, you're probably looking at a structural problem, not a bridge.

"…the draw is paid from cash reserves or personal savings. It is not rolled forward. It is not refinanced. The next month's expenses must come from operating revenue alone."
